# University Financial Regulations: Fee Structure, Deadlines, and Penalties
Document Reference: FIN-FEE-2026
Effective Academic Year: 2026-2027
Issued by: Office of the Chief Financial Officer (CFO)

## 1. Introduction and Financial Obligations
1.1 Enrollment at the University constitutes a legally binding financial agreement between the student and the institution. By registering for courses, utilizing university facilities, or accepting campus housing, the student agrees to be responsible for the timely payment of all assessed tuition, fees, and related charges.
1.2 The University utilizes electronic billing (e-billing) as the official means of distributing student financial statements. Invoices will be generated and distributed to the student's official university email address at least 30 days prior to the commencement of each semester.
1.3 Students are strictly responsible for checking their university email accounts regularly and ensuring that their financial obligations are met by the stipulated deadlines, regardless of whether a physical paper bill was received.
1.4 International students must ensure that all currency conversions and international wire transfers are initiated with sufficient lead time to clear the University's financial institution by the published deadlines. Bank processing delays will not be accepted as a valid justification for late payments.

## 3. Accepted Payment Methods
3.1 The University accepts the following methods of payment for tuition and fees:
- Electronic Bank Transfer (ACH) via the secure student portal.
- Domestic and International Wire Transfers (students must reference their Student ID number).
- Credit/Debit Cards (Visa, MasterCard, American Express). Please note that a 2.85% non-refundable convenience fee applies to all credit card transactions.
- Official Certified Bank Checks or Money Orders, payable to the University.
- Third-party sponsor billing (e.g., government agencies, corporate sponsors, military benefits). Students must submit valid sponsorship letters to the Bursar's Office by the due date.

3.2 Personal checks are accepted, however, any check returned for insufficient funds (NSF) will result in a $50.00 returned check fee. Additionally, the University reserves the right to refuse future payments by personal check from the student.
3.3 Cash payments exceeding $10,000.00 are strictly prohibited in compliance with federal anti-money laundering regulations.

## 4. Late Payment Penalties
4.1 Timely payment is critical to the university's operational planning and resource allocation. Failure to clear the outstanding balance by the published due dates will trigger a series of administrative and financial penalties.
4.2 Late fee is calculated at 5% of outstanding dues per week of delay. This penalty is strictly enforced and is automatically calculated and applied by the billing system at 11:59 PM every Friday following the payment deadline.
4.3 In addition to the monetary penalties, a financial hold will be immediately placed on the student's account. This hold will restrict the student from:
- Registering for future semesters.
- Accessing official academic transcripts.
- Receiving their diploma upon graduation.
- Utilizing certain campus services, including library borrowing privileges and recreational facility access.
4.4 The university reserves the right to drop students from their enrolled courses for severe payment delinquency. A notification of intent to drop will be issued two weeks prior to the action being taken.

## 5. Refund Policies and Withdrawal Schedules
5.1 Students who choose to withdraw from the University or drop specific courses may be eligible for a refund of their tuition and fees, based strictly on the official date of withdrawal as processed by the Registrar's Office.
5.2 Non-attendance in classes does not constitute an official withdrawal and will not generate a refund.

**Standard 16-Week Semester Refund Schedule:**
| Time of Withdrawal | Refund Percentage (Tuition) | Refund Percentage (Fees) |
|---|---|---|
| Prior to the first day of classes | 100% | 100% |
| During Week 1 (Add/Drop Period) | 100% | 100% (except Health Insurance) |
| During Week 2 | 80% | 50% |
| During Week 3 | 60% | 0% |
| During Week 4 | 40% | 0% |
| After Week 4 | 0% | 0% |

5.3 Room and board charges are refunded on a daily prorated basis until the fourth week of the semester. After the fourth week, no refunds for housing or dining plans will be issued.
5.4 Students withdrawing due to documented medical emergencies or involuntary military mobilization may petition the Bursar for an exception to the standard refund schedule. Such petitions require extensive documentation and are reviewed on a case-by-case basis.

## 6. Financial Hardship & Payment Plans
6.1 The University recognizes that families may experience unforeseen financial difficulties. To assist in managing educational costs, the University offers an authorized Installment Payment Plan (IPP).
6.2 The IPP allows students to divide their semester balance into four equal monthly installments. There is a non-refundable enrollment fee of $75.00 per semester to participate in the plan.
6.3 To be eligible for the IPP, the student must apply before the standard payment deadline and must not have a history of default on previous university payment plans.
6.4 Students on approved payment plans who miss installment deadlines shall be charged a late penalty of 2% of the outstanding amount per week. This penalty is applied specifically to those enrolled in the IPP structure.
6.5 Defaulting on the IPP (missing two consecutive installments) will result in the immediate cancellation of the plan. The entire remaining balance will become immediately due and subject to standard collection procedures.

## 7. Scholarship Fee Adjustments
7.1 University-awarded scholarships, grants, and institutional financial aid are automatically credited to the student's account and applied directly against tuition and fee charges.
7.2 If a student's total financial aid exceeds their billable university charges, a refund check or direct deposit for the credit balance will be issued to the student within 14 business days of the funds disbursing to the account.
7.3 Scholarships awarded by external entities (private foundations, community organizations) must be reported to the Financial Aid Office. These funds will be credited to the account only upon actual receipt of the funds by the University.
7.4 If a student's enrollment status changes (e.g., dropping from full-time to part-time), their scholarship eligibility may be recalculated, potentially resulting in a reduction or complete revocation of the award. In such cases, the student is responsible for any resulting balance on their account.

## 8. Delinquent Accounts and Collections
8.1 Any tuition balance remaining unpaid following the published semester deadline will accrue a mandatory late-payment surcharge assessed at 2% of the total outstanding balance per week of delay until settled in full. Accounts that remain unpaid at the end of the academic semester will be classified as severely delinquent.
8.2 The University will make reasonable efforts to contact the student and arrange for payment of delinquent accounts. If these efforts fail, the University reserves the right to refer the account to external third-party collection agencies or specialized legal counsel.
8.3 Should an account be referred for external collection, the student assumes all responsibility for paying the collection agency fees, which may be up to 30% of the principal balance, as well as any associated legal fees and court costs.
8.4 The University may also report delinquent accounts to national credit bureaus, which will negatively impact the student's personal credit rating.

## 9. Appeals
9.1 Students have the right to dispute specific charges on their account if they believe a billing error has occurred. Disputes must be submitted in writing to the Student Accounts Manager within 30 days of the charge appearing on the statement.
9.2 Appeals regarding late fees will only be considered in cases of documented university error (e.g., misapplied payments, processing delays originating within the Bursar's Office) or extreme, documented circumstances (such as sudden hospitalization).
9.3 The decision of the Bursar regarding financial appeals is final.
