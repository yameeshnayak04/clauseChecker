# University Scholarship and Financial Aid Policy
Document Identifier: POL-SCH-2026-V1
Effective Date: August 1, 2026
Issuing Department: Office of Scholarships and Financial Aid (OSFA)

## 1. Preamble
1.1 The University is committed to fostering academic excellence and providing equitable access to higher education. This policy dictates the framework for the allocation, administration, renewal, and revocation of all university-administered scholarships, grants, and bursaries.
1.2 The objective of this policy is to ensure transparency, fairness, and consistency in the distribution of financial aid resources, recognizing both exceptional merit and demonstrated financial need.
1.3 The Office of Scholarships and Financial Aid (OSFA) is the sole authority responsible for administering the provisions of this policy. All decisions made by OSFA are subject to the regulations detailed herein and are overseen by the University Financial Aid Committee.

## 2. Types of Scholarships Available
2.1 **Merit-Based Scholarships:** Awarded to students who have demonstrated outstanding academic achievements in their previous studies. These include the Presidential Excellence Award, the Dean's Merit Scholarship, and departmental subject-specific awards.
2.2 **Need-Based Scholarships:** Designed for students from economically disadvantaged backgrounds. Eligibility is determined through a comprehensive assessment of the student's family income, assets, and financial obligations. The primary award in this category is the University Opportunity Grant.
2.3 **Sports and Athletics Scholarships:** Granted to student-athletes who exhibit exceptional talent and are recruited to represent the University in recognized intercollegiate sports. These awards are highly competitive and require ongoing participation in the specified athletic program.
2.4 **Departmental and Specialized Awards:** Specific faculties and departments may offer specialized scholarships funded by alumni endowments or industry partnerships (e.g., the Engineering Innovators Fellowship or the Fine Arts Portfolio Grant). These are governed by the criteria set by the respective donors but administered through OSFA.
2.5 **Diversity and Inclusion Grants:** Designed to support students from underrepresented communities, fostering a diverse and inclusive campus environment.

## 3. Eligibility Criteria
3.1 To be considered for any university scholarship, a student must be officially admitted and enrolled as a degree-seeking student. Non-degree, visiting, or auditing students are ineligible.
3.2 Merit-based scholarship applicants must typically possess a minimum high school GPA of 3.8 (or equivalent) for entrance awards, or maintain a cumulative University GPA of 3.5 for continuing student awards.
3.3 Need-based scholarship applicants must submit the Free Application for Federal Student Aid (FAFSA) or the University's internal Financial Needs Assessment Form by the published deadline. International students must submit the International Student Financial Aid Application (ISFAA).
3.4 Students applying for sports scholarships must undergo athletic evaluations by the university's coaching staff and comply with the athletic association's amateurism and academic eligibility rules.
3.5 Providing false, misleading, or incomplete information on any scholarship application will result in immediate disqualification and potential referral for disciplinary action.

## 4. Application Process
4.1 The annual scholarship application cycle opens on October 1st and closes on March 1st for the subsequent academic year. Late applications will not be reviewed under any circumstances, barring a declared national emergency.
4.2 Prospective students are automatically considered for entrance merit scholarships based on their admissions application data. No separate application is required for these specific awards.
4.3 For need-based and specialized departmental awards, students must access the University Scholarship Portal, complete the comprehensive questionnaire, and upload all required supplemental documentation, such as tax transcripts, portfolios, or recommendation letters.
4.4 The Scholarship Selection Committee will review applications through a blind evaluation process where possible, ensuring that demographic biases are minimized. Award notifications will be issued electronically by May 15th.
4.5 Selected recipients must formally accept their award via the portal within two weeks of notification. Failure to do so will result in the forfeiture of the scholarship, which will then be awarded to the next eligible alternate.

## 5. Renewal Conditions
5.1 Most university scholarships are renewable for up to four consecutive academic years (eight semesters), provided the recipient consistently meets the renewal criteria.
5.2 **Academic Requirements (GPA):** Merit-based scholarship recipients must maintain a minimum cumulative GPA of 3.5. Need-based grant recipients must maintain satisfactory academic progress with a minimum cumulative GPA of 2.5.
5.3 **Credit Load Requirements:** Scholarship recipients must enroll in and successfully complete a minimum of 12 credit hours per semester (full-time status). Dropping below this threshold will jeopardize the award.
5.4 **Attendance and Conduct:** Recipients are expected to maintain excellent attendance records, as stipulated in the University Attendance Policy, and must not violate the University Code of Student Conduct. Any formal disciplinary action on the student's record may trigger a review of their scholarship status.
5.5 **Athletic Participation:** Sports scholarship recipients must remain in good standing with their respective teams, actively participate in practices and competitions, and maintain the specific GPA required by the athletic association.

## 6. Revocation Conditions
6.1 The University reserves the right to suspend, reduce, or completely revoke a scholarship under the following conditions:
6.1.a Failure to meet the GPA, credit load, or specific renewal criteria outlined in Section 5.
6.1.b Discovery of fraud, misrepresentation, or omission of material facts in the original application or renewal materials.
6.1.c Formal suspension or expulsion from the University due to academic or behavioral misconduct.
6.1.d Significant improvement in the student's financial situation, rendering them ineligible for need-based aid (applicable to need-based grants only).
6.1.e Voluntary withdrawal from the University or an unauthorized leave of absence.
6.2 In the event of a scholarship revocation due to fraud or misconduct, the student may be required to repay previously disbursed funds to the University.
6.3 The OSFA will send a formal 'Notice of Revocation' via certified email, outlining the reasons for the action and the effective date of the cancellation.

## 7. Appeal Process
7.1 Students whose scholarships have been revoked or not renewed due to failing academic criteria may submit a formal appeal if extenuating circumstances impacted their performance.
7.2 Valid grounds for appeal include severe personal illness, the death of an immediate family member, or significant, unforeseen financial distress.
7.3 Appeals must be submitted to the OSFA Appeals Committee within 14 calendar days of receiving the Notice of Revocation. The appeal packet must contain a detailed letter of explanation, an academic recovery plan approved by an academic advisor, and verifiable supporting documentation (e.g., medical records, death certificates).
7.4 The Appeals Committee convenes once per semester to review cases. The committee may reinstate the scholarship fully, place the student on a one-semester "Scholarship Probation," or uphold the revocation.
7.5 If a student is placed on Scholarship Probation, they will receive their funding for one additional semester, during which they must meet specific, stringent academic goals to regain full eligibility.
7.6 The decision of the Appeals Committee is final and binding within the University.

## 8. Special Provisions
8.1 **Transfer Students:** Transfer students are eligible for specialized transfer scholarships. Their eligibility is based on their cumulative GPA from their previous accredited institutions. Transfer scholarships are typically renewable for a maximum of two or three years, depending on the number of credits transferred.
8.2 **Part-Time Students:** While standard scholarships require full-time enrollment, the University offers a limited pool of "Non-Traditional Learner Grants" specifically for part-time, adult, and continuing education students. These are prorated based on the number of enrolled credits.
8.3 **Study Abroad:** University-funded scholarships may be applied to approved, credit-bearing study abroad programs. However, scholarships specifically designated for on-campus housing or dining cannot be used for overseas expenses. Students must consult with an OSFA counselor prior to departing for a study abroad program.
8.4 **Tax Implications:** Depending on local and national tax laws, portions of a scholarship utilized for room, board, or travel may be considered taxable income. It is the sole responsibility of the student to report such income accurately. The University does not provide tax advice.

## 9. Endowment and Donor Relations
9.1 Recipients of named, donor-funded scholarships are required to fulfill specific stewardship obligations as a condition of their award.
9.2 These obligations include writing an annual letter of gratitude to the donor, summarizing the student's academic progress and career aspirations.
9.3 Recipients may be requested to attend annual scholarship banquets or donor recognition events to personally interact with the benefactors supporting their education. Failure to participate in required stewardship activities may impact the renewal of the award.
