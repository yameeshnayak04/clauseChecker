# University Academic Regulations: Attendance Policy
Document Control Number: UAR-ATT-2026-V4
Effective Date: September 2026
Review Date: September 2029
Approving Authority: University Academic Senate

## 1. Preamble and Scope
1.1 The University recognizes that regular attendance and active participation in academic activities are fundamental to achieving the educational objectives of all programs. This policy establishes the requirements, procedures, and expectations regarding student attendance across all undergraduate and postgraduate programs.
1.2 The scope of this policy includes all instructional formats, including but not limited to lectures, tutorials, laboratory sessions, practicals, clinical placements, fieldwork, and seminars.
1.3 It is the primary responsibility of the student to ensure their attendance is accurately recorded and to communicate any absences promptly to the relevant academic personnel in accordance with the procedures outlined herein.
1.4 Faculty members are responsible for maintaining accurate attendance records, monitoring student engagement, and reporting prolonged or unexplained absences to the relevant academic advisors and departmental heads.
1.5 This policy supersedes all prior attendance regulations and guidelines. In the event of any discrepancies between departmental guidelines and this centralized policy, the terms detailed within this document shall prevail unless specific exemptions have been granted by the University Academic Senate.

## 2. General Attendance Requirements
2.1 All enrolled students are expected to attend 100% of their scheduled academic sessions to maximize their learning outcomes and fully benefit from the educational resources provided by the University.
2.2 The University utilizes a digital attendance tracking system. Students must record their presence using their authorized University ID card at the designated card readers located in each teaching venue within the first fifteen minutes of the scheduled class time.
2.3 Any student who fails to register their attendance via the digital system within the stipulated time frame will be marked as absent, irrespective of their physical presence in the venue, unless authorized by the instructor due to technical failures.
2.4 Instructors may, at their discretion, conduct secondary manual attendance checks to verify the accuracy of the digital records. Instances of fraudulent attendance registration (e.g., using another student's ID card) are considered serious academic misconduct and will be subjected to disciplinary action under the University Code of Conduct.
2.5 For online or hybrid courses, attendance is measured by logging into the learning management system, participating in live synchronous sessions, and completing continuous assessment tasks within the defined weekly deadlines.

## 3. Thresholds for Examination Eligibility
3.1 Students must maintain at least 75% attendance to be eligible to sit the end-semester examination for any given course. This threshold applies independently to each enrolled course.
3.2 The 75% attendance requirement is calculated based on the total number of scheduled contact hours for the course, encompassing lectures, tutorials, and laboratory sessions combined, unless specified otherwise in the course syllabus.
3.3 Students falling below the 75% threshold will be awarded an 'FA' (Failure due to Attendance) grade for the course. Consequently, they will be barred from participating in the final assessments and will be required to repeat the course in a subsequent semester, incurring applicable tuition fees.
3.4 The Office of the Registrar will publish interim attendance reports at the mid-semester point to warn students who are at risk of falling below the required threshold, allowing them time to rectify their attendance patterns.
3.5 It is the student's responsibility to monitor their own attendance through the university portal and report any discrepancies to the respective instructor within five working days of the class in question.

## 4. Leave and Absence Procedures
4.1 The University distinguishes between authorized and unauthorized absences. Only authorized absences are excluded from the calculation of the total required attendance, up to the limits specified in this policy.
4.2 Short-term absences (one to three consecutive days) for personal reasons must be communicated to the course instructor in writing prior to the commencement of the class. Instructors may require supplementary documentation to substantiate the absence.
4.3 Extended absences (four or more consecutive days) require a formal Leave of Absence application submitted to the Head of Department. Such applications must be accompanied by relevant supporting evidence and submitted within 48 hours of returning to campus.
4.4 Leaves of absence for participation in university-sponsored extracurricular activities, including national/international sports competitions, academic conferences, or authorized field trips, will be considered authorized provided prior approval has been obtained from the Dean of Students.
4.5 Bereavement leave for the loss of an immediate family member is granted for up to five consecutive working days. Students must submit appropriate documentation (e.g., death certificate, obituary) to the Student Affairs Office upon their return.

## 5. Medical Absence Provisions
5.1 The University recognizes that unforeseen health issues may impact a student's ability to maintain regular attendance. All medical absences must be supported by a valid medical certificate issued by a registered medical practitioner.
5.2 Students with documented medical absences shall be eligible for examination provided they maintain a minimum of 60% attendance, with the shortfall covered by approved medical certificates. This applies specifically to absences directly resulting from hospitalization, severe illness, or communicable diseases requiring isolation.
5.3 Medical certificates must clearly state the nature of the illness (where appropriate and not breaching confidentiality), the dates of recommended isolation or rest, and must be submitted to the University Health Center for verification within five working days of the student's return to academic activities.
5.4 The University Health Center reserves the right to reject medical certificates that are deemed suspicious, improperly formatted, or issued by practitioners not recognized by the relevant national medical council.
5.5 In cases of chronic illnesses or long-term disabilities that frequently affect attendance, students are advised to register with the Office of Disability Services at the beginning of the academic year to arrange for individualized reasonable accommodations.

## 6. Consequences of Non-Compliance
6.1 Failure to adhere to the attendance regulations outlined in this policy will result in escalating academic penalties, starting from formal written warnings to the aforementioned 'FA' grade assignment.
6.2 First Offense Warning: If a student's attendance drops below 85% without authorized reasons, an automated warning will be issued to the student's official university email address, with a copy sent to their designated academic advisor.
6.3 Academic Probation: Students who receive 'FA' grades in more than two courses within a single academic year will be placed on academic probation. Continued non-compliance during the probationary period may lead to academic suspension for a minimum of one semester.
6.4 Revocation of Privileges: Non-compliant students may lose their eligibility for university scholarships, campus housing, and participation in official university representative teams or student council positions.
6.5 Re-admission Provisions: Students who have been suspended due to severe attendance deficiencies must submit a formal petition for re-admission to the Admissions Office. The petition must include a detailed action plan demonstrating the student's commitment to improving their attendance and academic engagement.

## 7. Committee Powers and Exceptional Circumstances
7.1 The Academic Standing Committee, chaired by the Provost, is the final arbiter for complex academic matters, including appeals related to attendance requirements. The committee is empowered to waive attendance requirements entirely in exceptional circumstances, such as severe life-threatening illnesses, national emergencies, or other extraordinary events beyond the student's control, overriding any other minimum threshold.
7.2 Students seeking such exceptional waivers must file an appeal with the Academic Standing Committee within two weeks of receiving their final attendance shortage notification. The appeal must include comprehensive, verifiable documentation detailing the extraordinary nature of the circumstances.
7.3 The committee's decisions are binding and final. The committee may prescribe alternative academic exercises, additional assignments, or delayed assessment schedules as conditions for granting the attendance waiver.
7.4 The Academic Standing Committee meets bi-weekly during the semester and ad-hoc during the examination period to ensure timely resolution of urgent cases.
7.5 All waivers granted by the committee are documented in the student's permanent academic record but will not be reflected on the official academic transcript provided to external entities.

## 8. Record Keeping and Data Management
8.1 The Office of Information Technology is responsible for the maintenance, security, and functional integrity of the digital attendance tracking system.
8.2 Attendance data is classified as confidential student records and is handled in strict compliance with the University's Data Protection and Privacy Policy. Access to detailed attendance records is restricted to authorized faculty, academic advisors, and administrative personnel with a legitimate educational interest.
8.3 Attendance records will be retained for a period of five years following the student's graduation or formal withdrawal from the University, after which they will be permanently purged from the active databases and securely archived.
8.4 Students have the right to request a complete transcript of their attendance records through the University portal at any time during their enrollment. Any disputes regarding the accuracy of these records must follow the procedures outlined in Section 3.5.

## 9. Implementation and Review Guidelines
9.1 Deans of respective faculties are responsible for the uniform implementation of this policy across all departments within their jurisdiction.
9.2 The University Academic Senate will conduct a comprehensive review of this policy every three years to assess its effectiveness, fairness, and alignment with evolving pedagogical models and accreditation standards.
9.3 Stakeholder feedback, including input from faculty assemblies and the Student Union, will be actively sought and considered during the periodic review process.
9.4 Interim amendments to specific clauses may be enacted by the Provost in response to emergent legislative changes or public health directives. Such interim amendments will be communicated promptly to the university community and ratified by the Senate at its next scheduled convening.
9.5 Training sessions on the attendance policy and the digital tracking system will be provided to all new faculty and students during their respective orientation programs.

## 10. Appeals and Grievances
10.1 Beyond the exceptional circumstances outlined in Section 7, students may appeal standard attendance-related penalties (such as the assignment of an 'FA' grade) through the standard academic grievance process.
10.2 The initial step of the grievance process involves a formal meeting with the course instructor and the Head of Department. If the issue is not resolved at this level, the student may escalate the grievance to the Faculty Appeals Board.
10.3 Appeals based solely on ignorance of the attendance policy or dissatisfaction with its stringent nature will be summarily dismissed. Valid grounds for appeal are strictly limited to administrative errors in record keeping, demonstrated bias, or failure of the university personnel to adhere to the procedures stipulated in this document.
10.4 The timeline for submitting standard appeals is five working days from the date the penalty was officially communicated to the student. Late appeals will not be entertained unless the student can demonstrate that extenuating circumstances prevented timely submission.
